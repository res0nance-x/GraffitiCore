What is Graffiti?

Graffiti is a secure, decentralized, peer-to-peer (P2P) messaging and media sharing application. It allows you to communicate directly with other nodes across local networks or the internet without relying on central servers, corporate cloud providers, or third-party databases. All communications are encrypted end-to-end using public key cryptography, ensuring that only the intended recipients can read your messages and files.

Topic Identities vs. Direct Peers

Graffiti offers two primary ways to direct messages: to a Topic Identity or directly to a Peer's Public Key.

The standard and recommended way to communicate on Graffiti is via Topic Identities: My Identity → Shared Topic Identity → Friend's Identity

Why We Use Topic Identities

In public key encryption, when you encrypt a message directly to your friend's public key (a Peer), only their private key can decrypt it. As a result, you cannot read the messages you have sent unless the client creates and tracks redundant local copies.

Graffiti solves this cleanly through deterministic Topic Identities:

  • Deterministic Seeds: Identities can be created repeatably on any device using a secret "seed phrase" (a password or passphrase). For example, a group of friends discussing scooters can create a shared seed phrase such as "sportscooters BJr93077".

  • Unique Avatar & Alias: This seed generates a shared Identity with an alias and a unique avatar fingerprint:

    Alias: evertor

  • Group Forum: Share the seed phrase out-of-band (in person, over the phone, or via secure email). Everyone with the seed imports the Topic Identity and can immediately participate in the forum.

  • Readable Sent History: Because you hold the topic key, you can decrypt and read all messages in the topic—including your own sent messages.

  • Effortless Relaying: Relays can cache and forward messages for a shared topic without needing to know the identities or keys of everyone subscribed to it.

  • Clear Authorship: Messages sent to a topic are digitally signed by your personal sender identity. Friends always recognize who wrote each message by your unique personal alias and avatar.

  • Private Conversations: For 1-on-1 chats, you can either share a private two-person topic seed or send directly to your friend's Peer public key.

The Net Tab (Network & Connections)

The Net tab manages your node's server status, network discovery, and active peer connections.

  • My Node: Displays your device's node name, listening port, and unique device avatar. Friends use this avatar to visually recognize and reconnect to your specific server.

  • Server Mode: Determines whether you accept incoming connections from other peers. Starting the node server opens a local port (typically 38214). You can always connect outbound to others as a client without running a server.

  • Relay Mode: Controls message forwarding. In standard client mode, your node only downloads messages intended for identities you currently hold. Enabling Relay mode allows your node to act as a store-and-forward peer, caching encrypted payloads for any network identity and forwarding them to peers upon synchronization.

  • Nearby Nodes & Discovery: Automatically discovers Graffiti nodes on your local area network.

    • Multicast Discovery: Very fast and the preferred method for discovering local peers automatically on startup or via manual discovery.
    • Full IP Scan: Scans the entire local subnet. Takes longer, but finds devices on networks where multicast traffic is blocked or unsupported.

  • Active Connections: Displays all currently connected nodes with their IP address, port, peer key, and connection direction (inbound when acting as a server, or outbound when acting as a client).

  • Manual Peer Connection: Connect directly to any peer by entering their IP address (or hostname) and port. This is useful when discovery is disabled or when connecting to a remote node across the internet. Nodes on the public internet must have a publicly reachable IP and open port; Graffiti does not perform NAT traversal.

The Messages Tab (Composing & Media)

The Messages tab is your central communication hub. The message timeline is filtered by the recipient selected in the To field.

  • From & To Selection:

    • From: The personal Identity you want to send as. Your friends will see this identity's alias and avatar as the message author.
    • To: The recipient topic Identity or peer public key. Selecting a conversation loads its message history.

  • Rich Markdown Support: You can format message text using Markdown:

    • Headers (#, ##, ###)
    • Emphasis (bold, italic, strikethrough )
    • Lists (ordered 1. and unordered *, -)
    • Quotes (> blockquote)
    • Code (`inline code` and ``` fenced code blocks)
    • Links ([Title](url) and automatic URL detection)

  • Sending Files & Attachments:

    • PC / Desktop: Click the Send File button to select one or multiple files, drag-and-drop files or folders directly onto the message timeline, or paste files/images directly from your clipboard (Ctrl+V). Selecting, dropping, or pasting multiple files automatically packages them into a compact .pack archive.
    • Android / Mobile: Use the Send File button (with multi-file selection support), or use your device's system Share menu from any app (gallery, file manager, etc.) to share files directly into Graffiti. Sharing multiple files automatically bundles them into a .pack archive.

  • Inline Media Viewing: Graffiti automatically identifies and previews recognized file types:

    • Text / Code / Logs: Rendered inline with syntax blocks; large files can be expanded in the full-screen Content Viewer.
    • Images: Rendered inline with full-screen pinch, zoom, and pan controls.
    • Audio & Video: Played directly within the application using the integrated browser player.
    • Pack Archives: Explored and streamed directly within the application.

  • Message Context Menu Actions: Right-click any message on desktop (or long-press on mobile touch devices) to open a contextual action menu with convenient message operations:

    • View in Full Viewer: Opens the message content or attachment in the full-screen Content Viewer for detailed reading, zoomable image inspection, media playback, or exploring pack archives.
    • Forward: Re-encrypts and forwards the message or attachment to another Topic Identity or Peer contact using your currently selected sender identity.
    • Quote: Quotes the message content into the message composition box, preformatted as a Markdown blockquote, allowing you to reply directly with context.
    • Copy: Copies the message text directly to your clipboard (available for text-based messages).
    • Download: Downloads the attached file or message payload directly to your device storage.
    • Delete: Permanently deletes the message and its local payload from your node's storage.

Pack Files (.pack & .epack)

A Pack file is an open-source archive format engineered specifically for in-place streaming, seeking, and encrypted streaming (.epack) without requiring temporary extraction to disk.

  • Instant Streaming & Seeking: Unlike standard ZIP or TAR archives that must be fully unpacked, Pack files support random position seeking. You can instantly jump forward and backward in multi-gigabyte video or audio files without waiting for a full file download or decryption.

  • Encrypted Packs (.epack): Encrypted with a password and decrypted on-the-fly in memory as data blocks are read.

  • Rich Collections: A pack file can bundle an entire website, interactive web app, photo gallery, or music album into a single portable package.

  • Pack Tools & Source Code: Graffiti includes built-in Pack creation for multi-file attachments. For advanced pack authoring, encryption, and custom archives, you can also use the dedicated PackManager desktop tool:

    • PackManager Web Overview & Download - https://www.res0nance.cc/packmanager/index.html
    • PackManager GitHub Repository - https://github.com/res0nance-x/PackManager
    • PackViewer GitHub Repository - https://github.com/res0nance-x/PackViewer

The Identities Tab (Personal & Topic Keys)

Accounts in Graffiti are called Identities. The Identities tab is where you create, import, and manage both personal author identities and shared topic identities.

  • Seed Phrases: Identities are generated deterministically from a secret seed phrase (passphrase). This seed functions like a master cryptographic key. Anyone who learns or guesses your seed phrase can read your messages and impersonate that identity. Always use long, unique, unguessable seed phrases for private communications.

  • Aliases vs. Avatars: Each identity has:

    • A text Alias: convenient for human reference, but not guaranteed to be globally unique.
    • An Avatar shape and color palette: mathematically generated directly from the public key, guaranteeing a unique visual fingerprint for every identity.

  • Saved vs. Ephemeral Identities:

    • Saved to Disk: Stored locally in encrypted app storage for everyday convenience across restarts.
    • Ephemeral: Held strictly in volatile memory and wiped as soon as Graffiti is closed. This provides maximum security for sensitive conversations, though you will need to re-enter your seed phrase the next time you open the app. You can mix and match saved and ephemeral identities on the same node.

The Peers Tab (Friends & Assisted Relaying)

The Peers tab manages the public keys of your friends and contacts without needing their seed phrases.

Because of Graffiti's topic identity model, you may not always need to manage individual peers. However, importing a friend's public key provides two important capabilities:

  1. Direct One-on-One Messaging: You can address messages directly to your friend's public key. The message is encrypted so that only their private key can read it (meaning you will not be able to read your sent copies).
  2. Peer-Assisted Caching: Even when you are not running in full Relay mode, your node will automatically request and cache messages on behalf of any Peers you hold whenever you synchronize with relays or peers. When you later connect with your friend, your node automatically serves those cached messages to them.

The Settings Tab (Appearance, Audio & Storage)

The Settings tab lets you customize the user interface, sound alerts, and storage limits.

  • Appearance: Choose from curated color themes (Dark, Slate, Amber, Forest, Cyberpunk, and Light), adjust the application font size, and customize font families.
  • Notification Sounds: Select custom bell notification sounds for incoming messages, preview sound playback, and configure options to ignore urgent message alerts.
  • Global Storage Quota: Set a maximum storage threshold for cached messages and media. When total storage exceeds this quota, Graffiti automatically prunes older cached content until usage drops below the limit.
  • Per-Identity Storage Pruning: Manage storage footprint per identity. You can prune the older 50% of messages for a specific identity or clear its message history entirely.
  • About & Documentation: Check the current software version / build date and open this documentation viewer.
